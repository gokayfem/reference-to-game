# Lessons from four finished games

These cases informed the workflow before this packaged skill existed. They are not a with/without-skill evaluation and should not supply defaults for unrelated inputs.

**Jetboat:** orange hero, yellow life vests, turquoise water and red sandstone establish recognizable color relationships. Isolated boat/crew references → generated meshes preserved the subject. A varied canyon kit, painted foliage and a water/wake pass mattered more than adding courses. A swept collision model and ordered checkpoints made the attractive scene a race. Remaining gaps include spray shape and some camera/terrain composition.

**Jellyworks:** the cream/mint UI, candy translucency and tactile machinery carried the style. Generated room art and small icons complemented state-driven modular geometry. Full generated machine meshes were evaluated and rejected when they did not fit runtime needs. A deterministic port graph, buffers and one-time deliveries prevented a visually plausible but logically broken factory. Transmission quality required a measured fallback.

**Petal Pals:** painted facing views and animation strips on a 2.5D scene preserved the illustration better than a generic 3D character. Consistent baselines, directional animation, occlusion and collision still required implementation. Stable IDs kept exactly three companions through maps, journals, saves and restart. The paper UI and foreground framing were essential to the look.

**Train Battle:** blue train surfaces, orange rails, purple robots and white/orange weapon create strong visual roles. A train-local coordinate frame simplified collision while scenery moved. Mesh cleanup, hand poses and weapon sockets needed in-engine review. A successful compile did not prove a reachable six-car sequence; input-driven completion and retry did.

Across cases: a first scene comparison exposed color/camera/material errors early; accepted assets were reused; generated footage was treated as evidence with defects; additional polish targeted the largest mismatch; recordings showed actual input-driven state changes. The general workflow is portable. The daylight setting, saturation, scene counts and controls are not.
